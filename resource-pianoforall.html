PianoForAll — an honest look

TitanCore Resources ShelfPrices checked November 2026

Affiliate disclosure: TitanCore Holdings may earn a commission if you buy through links on this page, at no extra cost to you. We only list products we've reviewed against our standards, and we say what we don't like, too.

What it is. PianoForAll is a self-paced piano course created by teacher Robin Hall and sold online since 2006. Instead of starting with sheet-music drills, it teaches chords, rhythm, and playing by ear first, then adds reading music gradually. The path runs from rhythm and party style through blues and rock 'n' roll, chord inversions, ballads, and jazz, with an introduction to classical pieces along the way. It is delivered as downloadable interactive ebooks with video and audio built into the pages, and the maker also offers a newer online player.

Who it fits. Adults who always meant to learn, returning players who quit the traditional way, and parents who want a structured start for an older child or teenager. Who should skip it: anyone preparing for graded exams or formal classical training — this course will not get you there, and a qualified local teacher will serve you better. Also skip it if you know you only stay motivated when an app listens to you play and corrects you in real time. PianoForAll doesn't do that.

How it works. Ten ebooks — nine core books plus a bonus speed-learning volume — with roughly 300 short video lessons and hundreds of audio examples placed next to the exercises. Counts vary a little between the maker's pages and editions, so treat the totals as approximate. On Windows and Android the ebooks open in the free Kotobee Reader; on Mac, iPhone, and iPad they open in Apple Books; PDF versions are included. The media files can be large, so download on Wi-Fi. There is a free Test Drive — a few sample videos and a lesson — that needs no card, and we suggest starting there. Once downloaded, the lessons work offline and can be printed.

What we liked. The teaching order. Chords and rhythm before notation means beginners are playing recognizable music in the first week, which is exactly when most people quit other courses. The one-time price is the other strength: subscription piano apps commonly charge more per year than this course costs once. Offline files, the free test drive, and a track record going back to 2006 round it out.

What's weak. The presentation is plain next to glossy subscription apps, and there is no interactive feedback — nothing hears your playing or flags a wrong note. The ebook setup also asks a few minutes of patience, and the large media files need room on your device. After checkout you may be offered an optional "Classics By Ear" add-on ($49) aimed at classical pieces; the core course stands on its own, and nobody needs the add-on to finish it.

The claims line. The maker advertises a faster, more enjoyable road to playing. We don't promise how fast anyone learns — that part is practice. What we can stand behind is what's in the box, described above.

Price and guarantee. $49 as a one-time payment, as shown on the maker's order page when we checked in November 2026 (listed as reduced from $79) — no subscription. Prices change; the figure that counts is the one on the order page when you buy. Every purchase carries a 60-day money-back guarantee.

Bottom line. A complete, modestly priced beginner course with an honest guarantee and an unusual but sensible teaching order. If you decide to buy, the button below supports the free resources on this site at no extra cost to you.
